%Same as the main doc - Safe and efficient RL
Long-horizon tasks are important for robotics and modern AI, and reinforcement learning (RL)
is a general way to solve them without hand-designed solutions. Such tasks can naturally be broken
into a sequence of subgoals. Value Disaggregation (VaDaR) learns a separate critic for each subgoal
and trains a single policy to maximize a weighted combination of critic values, restricted at each
step to subgoals not yet completed. However, the online exploration VaDaR relies on is still unsafe
in two distinct ways: kinematic safety, the physical limits on the robot’s own configuration that must
never be crossed regardless of task, such as self-collision and joint limits, and semantic safety, the
behavioral limits specific to the task being performed, such as not spilling the contents of a container.
We extend VaDaR with dedicated safety heads: additional critics that, unlike task subgoal critics,
remain persistently weighted into the policy objective rather than being switched in and out with task
progress, so that safety functions as a standing subgoal rather than a stage-specific one. Moreover, we
hypothesize that VaDaR with safety heads (1) is more sample-efficient than VaDaR alone, since the
safety heads supply dense learning signal beyond sparse task reward, and (2) explores more safely
throughout training, since the policy is biased away from unsafe behavior from the outset rather
than only after convergence. In particular, we run analysis experiments comparing architectures for
the safety critic heads and strategies for aggregating safety and task critics into the policy objective.
Separately, we compare safety critics derived from offline Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) reachability analysis
against safety critics learned online from sparse negative reward on unsafe events. Taken together,
