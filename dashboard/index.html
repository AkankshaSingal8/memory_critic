What BoN already does in OGPO

sample_actions_BON in OGPO_public draws 8 chunks from the EMA actor with the SDE sampler. It scores each chunk with the lower of 2 members drawn at random from a 10-member Q ensemble and keeps the best. The OGPO SDE curves on the Results tab went through it, and the ODE curves did not. A memory critic swaps out only that scoring step.

The BC phase never trains the critic, but its SDE eval still calls this BoN. The SDE curves for "OGPO repo, BC phase only" are therefore a pick among 8 made by a random critic, close to plain SDE sampling with one sample.

IMPRINT itself never uses memory as a critic. Its only value-like part is the Stage-1 progress head, which reads the memory tokens with no action input. It can't tell 8 candidates apart, so the memory critic adds the action as a separate input: Q(s_t, m_t, a). Candidate chunks never enter the encoder, which was trained on executed actions only.

Step one: train the no-memory critic (A3)

Built on 27 Oct with changes: it ran on the flow-BC policies with 8-step chunks and pure Monte Carlo targets. The Rollouts and critic tab has what actually ran. It was meant to come before anything touches IMPRINT. The memory and clock critics reuse its data, targets and training loop and add one input each. OGPO_public already has the training step; the new code is the rollouts, the dataset builder and the swap into BoN.

Training on the checkpoint's replay buffer skips the rollouts and makes a quick pipeline test. That buffer mixes every policy from training, though, so it doesn't count as A3.

Which policies, and how much room there is

Only the OGPO runs saved checkpoints: actor, critic and replay buffer for square ph and mh at every demo count, seeds 0, 7 and 42. The OGPO BC-only sweep saved nothing, and this repo's flow-BC checkpoints use a different actor with no critic. BoN on a pure BC policy therefore needs a BC rerun with saving turned on, or a wrapper for those flow-BC checkpoints. That wrapper now exists (collect_flow_bc_rollouts.py --bon_n); see the Rollouts and critic tab.

On the full datasets, OGPO already ends at — ODE and — SDE on ph, and — and — on mh, which leaves little to rerank. The policy only sees the 23-dim obs, so history can help the critic only through what that obs leaves out (velocities, contact, a failed grasp the object pose no longer shows) and through the time cap. Start with the 10 and 20-demo checkpoints, where critics are weaker and failures more common.

Order of work

  1. Load each OGPO final and run A0, A1 and A2 with a standalone eval script. Check: A2 matches the W&B SDE final within noise, and A2 beats A1 somewhere.
  2. Train the no-memory critic (A3) as in the section above, and run it in BoN next to A1 and A2. Check: on held-out episodes it fits the returns at least as well as the checkpoint's critic. Status: trained on the flow-BC policies; BoN eval submitted 27 Oct.
  3. Reuse the A3 rollouts for the memory work, and cut the replay buffers into episodes as extra Stage-1 data. Check: the rollouts hold enough failures to learn from.
  4. Build a low-dim IMPRINT store (object vector as a one-token visual stream, so no rendering) and train Stage 1 once on one L40S. Check: reconstruction of past actions and proprio beats a no-history baseline.
  5. Freeze the encoder, cache m_t for every step, and train A3, A4 and A5 with identical configs.
  6. Offline checks on held-out episodes: success AUC of Q at matched steps, and how often A5 picks the same candidate as A3. Optionally, restore sim states and roll out all 8 candidates to get true rankings. Check: A5 ranks differently from A3. If not, the online result will be the same, so stop here.
  7. Memory server in .venv-imprint plus the online BoN eval in OGPO's env, with the same reset seeds and actor noise in every arm, 256 episodes per checkpoint and arm. Check: A5 beats both A3 and A4 at 10 and 20 demos by more than the spread across seeds. If not, move to a task where history carries more (images only, occlusion, tool_hang).
  8. Add the BoN results to this dashboard.

Open questions

  1. Start with the OGPO finals at 10 and 20 demos (ph and mh, 3 seeds)? That's the recommendation.
  2. Should pure BC policies get BoN too? Both options need extra work (rerun or wrapper).
  3. Low-dim memory only at first, or render images during the new rollouts so an image memory can follow without collecting again?
  4. Monte Carlo targets (simple, on-policy) or TD targets (closer to how the checkpoint critic was trained)?

How one rollout runs

The stepping loop is a copy of the eval's evaluate_qipm_parallel without the Q tracking. Env and agent come from the eval's own builders in eval_bc_run.py, so the controller, dataset root and flags match the eval.

Per env, per step

ResetEpisode e starts from seed 10,000,000 + e.
Queue empty?Then the SDE actor reads the obs and returns a chunk of 8 actions. This step gets chunk_pos 0.
Pop one actionClip to [−1, 1]. The clipped action is what gets saved.
Step and recordSave obs, action, reward, next obs, flags, ids.
Repeat until success or step 400. A finished env sits idle until all 8 in the batch are done.

Policies

  • 30 runs from train_flow_bc.sbatch: square ph and mh, 10/20/50/100/200 demos, seeds 0/7/42.
  • Each uses selected/target_100.pkl, the step with the best 3-point smoothed ODE success.
  • The actor sees the 23-dim low-dim state and outputs 8 actions of 7 dims each.
  • Name: square_{ph|mh}_{N}demos_seed{S}_sde. The source step is in the JSON next to each file.

Sampling

  • SDE (agent.sample_actions): target actor plus noise net, one sample per decision, no best-of-N.
  • It is the same sampler as the eval_sde curves on the Results tab.
  • SDE over ODE because a critic needs different outcomes from the same start. ODE varies only through its initial noise.
  • Policy noise for batch b is fold_in(PRNGKey(1000), b). All 30 policies share base key 1000, and a resumed task draws the same noise.

Episodes and start states

  • 400 episodes per policy, in 50 batches of 8 parallel envs.
  • Episode e has the same start scene for every policy, so results pair up across demo counts.
  • Start seeds begin at 10,000,000. Eval seeds stop at 500,063, so no eval scene is reused.
  • An episode ends on success (terminated) or at 400 steps (truncated).
  • Autoreset is off, so the last next_obs is the true final state.

What each file holds

  • <policy_id>.npz plus .json metadata, in /data/user_data/asingal2/memory_critic/rollouts/flow_bc_target100/.
  • Per step: obs, action, reward, next_obs, terminated, truncated, done, is_success, episode_id, timestep, chunk_pos, and 3 subtask flags.
  • Per episode: success, length, return, start seed.
  • If an episode succeeds partway through a chunk, the rest of that chunk never ran and is not saved.

Running and checking

  • Slurm array on preempt, one task per policy: CPU only, 10 cores, 24 GB.
  • On preemption the task stops between batches, requeues and skips the batches already on disk.
  • check_rollouts.py tests each file: step count equals the summed episode lengths, one done flag per episode, next_obs[t] == obs[t+1], chunk_pos == timestep mod 8, and success matches the last step.
  • Run on the eval's own seeds, the collector matched eval_sde within 64-episode noise.

How the critic is trained

One critic for all 30 policies, trained three times with critic seeds 0, 1 and 2. The network and optimizer are qipm's own critic settings; only the data and target are new.

What one training row is cut from

One training sample

Obs s_t23 dims, at a decision point.
Chunk a_t … a_t+7The 8 executed actions, flattened to 56 dims. Zeros past the episode end.
10 MLPsEach 4 hidden layers of 512 with LayerNorm. Same input, separate weights.
10 valuesQ_1 … Q_10, one scalar each.
TrainingEvery member regresses on G_t. Loss is the squared error averaged over members and batch.
ScoringThe ensemble mean is the critic's value.

Training loop, in plain English

  1. Collect rollouts. Run each of the 30 frozen policies for 400 episodes with the SDE sampler and save every step as (s, a, r, s′, terminated, truncated, episode id, timestep, chunk_pos). Call this D.
  2. Compute returns. In each episode, walk back from the last step: G = r at the last step, then Gt = rt + 0.99 · Gt+1. The last step is the end whether the episode succeeded or hit the 400-step cap. Nothing is added past it.
  3. Build rows. Keep the decision points (chunk_pos = 0). Each row gets input x = (st, at … at+7), 79 numbers with zeros for actions past the episode end, and target y = Gt.
  4. Split by episode. Episodes 0 to 287 train, 288 to 319 validate, 320 to 399 test.
  5. Initialise Q. 10 MLPs with random weights (critic seed 0, 1 or 2) and one AdamW optimizer, learning rate 3e-4.
  6. Repeat 200,000 times:
    1. Sample 256 training rows at random.
    2. Each MLP m predicts Qm(x).
    3. Loss = mean over the 10 MLPs and 256 rows of (Qm(x) − y)². The target y is the logged return and never comes from Q.
    4. Clip the gradient norm at 1000 and take one AdamW step.
    5. Every 5,000 steps, compute validation MSE with the ensemble mean. If it is the lowest so far, save the weights.
  7. Report. Load the saved weights. Q(x) is the mean of the 10 MLPs. Compute the test metrics on episodes 320 to 399.

A SARSA / TD version changes only step 6c: y = rt + 0.99 rt+1 + … + 0.997 rt+7 + 0.998 · Q̄(st+8, at+8 … at+15), with Q̄ a slowly updated copy of Q and the last term dropped when the episode has ended. Step 2 is then not needed.

Why Monte Carlo, not bootstrapping

  • The experiment spec starts with Monte Carlo return regression, L = E[(Q(s, a) − Gt)²].
  • Every episode in D runs to its end (success or the 400-step cap) under one fixed policy, so Gt is an unbiased sample of that policy's Q. Bootstrapping is not needed to get a target.
  • The target never depends on Q, so there is no target network and Q's errors do not feed back into its own targets.
  • The cost is variance: each row has one sampled future. The 400-step cap also counts as a real ending, which is where the bands in the Q against MC plot come from.
  • SARSA / TD on the logged next chunk is the next variant to try. The rollouts already hold everything it needs.

Episode split

Train: episodes 0 to 287 Val: 288 to 319, picks the checkpoint Test: 320 to 399, reported

Episode e starts from the same scene for every policy, so no test scene appears in training under any policy.

Samples

  • Only decision points, where best-of-N would score candidate chunks. --points all uses every step instead.
  • The action input is the chunk as the policy ran it, the same 56-dim shape the qipm critic takes.
  • All 30 policies are pooled, from 10-demo policies that mostly fail to 200-demo ones that mostly succeed.

Target

G_t = r_t + γ r_t+1 + γ² r_t+2 + … to the episode end, with γ = 0.99.

  • The logged reward is a subtask penalty: −1, −⅔ or −⅓ per step with 0, 1 or 2 of the 3 subtasks done, and 0 on the success step. G_t is the discounted sum of these to the episode end.
  • It ranges from 0 at the success step to about −98 for a full 400-step failure.
  • No bootstrap at the 400-step cap: a timeout counts as the end.
  • No TD term and no target network. It is plain regression on the return.

Optimizer and schedule

  • Gradient norm clipped at 1000, then AdamW: lr 3e-4, weight decay 1e-5, eps 1e-8, no schedule.
  • Batch 256, drawn with replacement from the train rows. 200k steps on one L40S.
  • Val and test MSE every 5k steps. The lowest val MSE is saved.
  • Train loss keeps falling to 200k while val MSE bottoms out much earlier, so the saved checkpoint, not the step budget, decides the critic.

Test metrics

  • Reported per group (ph or mh × demo count, the 3 policy seeds pooled), per demo count, and overall.
  • MSE against G_t, next to var_G, the MSE of always guessing the group mean. R² = 1 − MSE / var_G.
  • Bias: mean of Q − G.
  • Calibration: 10 quantile bins of Q. ECE is the count-weighted gap between mean Q and mean G per bin. Slope and intercept of G on Q should be 1 and 0.
  • Success AUC: how well Q at each episode's first decision predicts whether the episode succeeds.

Outputs

  • critics/mc_decision_seed{0,1,2}/: critic_best.pkl, metrics.json, test_predictions.npz.
  • plot_q_vs_mc.py draws Q against G per group from the test predictions, with a calibration line.
  • Curves are in W&B, project memory-critic, group critic_mc_nomem.

Use in best-of-N

  • make_bon_actor draws N SDE chunks per env, clips them, scores each with the ensemble mean and runs the top one.
  • OGPO's own BoN scores with the lower of 2 random members instead.
  • bon_eval.sbatch runs N = 1, 4, 8, 16, 32 on all 30 policies with the seed-0 critic, 128 episodes each.
  • Those episodes start from new seeds (20,000,000 + e), shared by every N, so N = 1 is a paired baseline. Submitted 27 Oct.

Where this differs from the plan

  • The policies are the 30 flow-BC checkpoints, not the OGPO finals.
  • Chunks are 8 actions (the flow-BC horizon), not 4.
  • The target is pure Monte Carlo. bc_q_update and its TD term are not used.
  • Data is 400 episodes from 8 envs per policy, not about 1000 from 32.
  • The held-out set is fixed episode indices (val 288 to 319, test 320 to 399), not a random tenth.
